## 1. The core idea, in one line

Ubuntu Heritage tells the real history of South Africa's places — in the voices of the people who
lived it or the literature that recorded it — and turns every story into a doorway to an actual
place you can go and stand in.

Learn about June 16 → find the Hector Pieterson Memorial, and what happened there. Learn about
Mandela → discover Vilakazi Street, where he lived, and the museums around it. History isn't a
static lesson here — it's an on-ramp to travel.

**Where we want to take it, and why we are writing to you:** that same doorway should end at a
booking with a real local operator. The app is built for it. What is missing is the operator
relationships — which is exactly what the Department can open.

## 2. What Ubuntu Heritage actually does

### a) Real history, place by place — not a forced narrative

Every story in Ubuntu Heritage is tied to a specific, real place. There is no single linear
storyline the user has to follow start to finish. A user can enter through whatever pulls them in —
a date, a person, an event, a topic — and explore outward from there. The history is accurate and
sourced; the structure is exploratory, matching how people actually engage with curiosity and
travel planning.

### b) Told by real people and real literature — never by AI

The history itself comes from two sources, and only these two:

- **Oral history from the people who hold it** — community members, elders, local historians —
  collected with full, explicit informed consent (POPIA-compliant) before any recording happens.
- **Existing published literature**, used only where the work is in the public domain or we hold
  clear licensing — not summarized or reworded in a way that misrepresents the source. This is
  enforced in code, not by policy: an unverified rights status blocks ingest and publication
  outright.

**No historical claim in Ubuntu Heritage is generated by AI.** Every fact traces to a named
contributor or a citable published source, and anything uncertain is marked as uncertain rather
than filled in. That is the integrity line, and it is the one that matters for credibility.

**Where AI is used today, and how it is disclosed.** Scene and location imagery is currently
AI-generated, and the app says so, everywhere it appears — each image carries an "Artistic
interpretation" label in all eleven supported languages, and the About the Sources screen states
plainly that scene images "are not historical photographs or depictions of real people." AI is also
used for translation and language accessibility (see §5). It is never used to author history, and
never to depict a real named person as though the image were a record of them.

**Where we intend to take it.** AI illustration is a starting position, not the destination. As
commissioned, licensed, and community-contributed artwork becomes available — and this is an area
where a partnership could directly help, by connecting us with heritage-site image archives and
local artists — that artwork replaces the generated imagery, story by story. The disclosure
labelling stays either way, so a user always knows what they are looking at.

### c) Every story points at a real place — and is built to point at a bookable one

This is the mechanism that makes Ubuntu Heritage a tourism product, not just an education product.
Each historical entry is tied to one or more **real-world, visitable locations**, and the app is
built so that each of those can carry a link to whoever offers a way to experience it.

**Built and live today:** 49 sourced heritage places across 18 cities, reachable from the map,
from a city, or from a story. **Not yet built:** the operator links themselves — see §3.

Illustrating the intended pairing:

| Learn about... | Would link to... | Today |
|---|---|---|
| June 16 / the Soweto Uprising | A guided bike tour through Soweto | The Hector Pieterson Memorial is live and sourced; no tour operator is linked yet |
| Nelson Mandela | Vilakazi Street, Mandela House | Both live and sourced, from the Soweto Heritage Trust's own record |
| A museum tied to an event or figure | The museum's visit information | Museums are live; ticketing links await partnerships |

The app does not tell you a story and leave you there — it tells you where it happened. Handing you
the next step, through a named local operator, is the part a partnership makes possible.

## 3. Why this is a tourism proposal, not just an app

Our ask to the Department of Tourism is built on this framing: **Ubuntu Heritage teaches people
about South Africa's history in a way that points them at the places it happened.** The intended
path is:

**Curiosity about history → a real place → a real local business → a visit.**

### Where that path actually reaches today

Ubuntu Heritage currently carries **49 heritage places across 18 cities**, each with a sourced
account of why it matters and a citation a reader can check. Tap a landmark and you learn what it
is, where it is, and who says so. That half is built and live.

**The last step — the booking — is not built yet, and this is what a partnership would unlock.** The
app has the model for a bookable experience and the surfaces to show one; what it does not have is
operator relationships. Introductions to heritage sites, tour operators and provincial boards are
precisely what would turn 49 sourced places into 49 doorways.

### One thing we will not do, and want to be plain about

**Ubuntu Heritage does not and will not track who went.**

Knowing that a particular person read about June 16 and then bought a Soweto tour means attaching an
identifier to them and following it out of the app. That is exactly the personal-data collection
POPIA exists to restrain, and it would apply to the children using our Kids mode. We have therefore
built the referral path so that it *cannot* carry a tracking parameter — a test fails the build if
one is ever added.

The honest consequence: we can tell you how many heritage places are live, how many operators are
linked, and how many of those links are verified and working. **We cannot report visits, bookings or
conversions, and we would have to rebuild the product's privacy posture to start.** If attribution
matters to the Department, that is a conversation with its own consent design — not a setting we can
switch on.

We think the trade is the right one. A heritage product that quietly follows its readers is not one
communities will trust with their history.

## 4. Why this complements existing tourism initiatives

South African Tourism's **Sho't Left** campaign has spent years building the infrastructure and
audience for domestic travel — deals, listings, a partner platform for tourism businesses, and a
proven message: South Africans and visitors alike often don't realize what's around them and need a
reason to go. Sho't Left's current content leans toward adventure, beach, and general "hidden gem"
deals; it does not currently center history or heritage as the reason to travel.

Ubuntu Heritage supplies exactly that missing reason. Where Sho't Left answers "where can I get a
good deal," Ubuntu Heritage answers "why should I go, and what does this place mean" — and is built
to route the reader onward into a bookable experience. The two are complementary, not competing:
Ubuntu Heritage can drive intent and discovery; existing platforms and local operators handle the
booking and the commercial relationship.

## 5. What supports this model technically (kept brief — not the focus of this pitch)

- **Offline-first app** (WatermelonDB + Supabase sync) — works in low-connectivity areas, relevant
  for travel use.
- **Language accessibility** — translation and indigenous-language support (Setswana, English, and
  others), so stories are accessible in the languages communities actually speak.
- **POPIA-compliant consent flow** for any oral history or personal data collected from
  contributors.
- **One codebase** (Expo/React Native) covering web, Android, and iOS.

## 6. The business/partnership layer

Each linked location or experience represents a potential partner relationship:

- **Tour operators** (e.g. Soweto bike tours) — referral or booking-integration partnerships.
- **Museums and heritage sites** (e.g. Hector Pieterson Museum, Mandela House) — visibility and
  visitor-routing partnerships, potentially co-branded content.
- **Local guides and community contributors** — a path to include and credit the people whose oral
  history populates the app, and potentially compensate them as contributors.

This gives the Department of Tourism a concrete story to tell: Ubuntu Heritage doesn't just promote
South Africa's history — it routes readers toward specific, named local heritage businesses and
community contributors.

*Routes, not measures.* We can report how many places and operators are linked and how many of those
links are verified and working. We cannot report how many people went, and §3 explains why we chose
not to be able to.

## 8. Suggested pilot scope

The places are already in place across 18 cities. What a pilot needs to prove is the half that is
not built — that a sourced story reliably delivers a visitor to a named operator.

We propose proving it in **Soweto first**, where the app is already deepest: Vilakazi Street, the
Hector Pieterson Memorial, Mandela House and Regina Mundi are live and sourced, and a single
operator relationship would complete the path end to end. Once it works there, it scales city by
city over content that already exists.
