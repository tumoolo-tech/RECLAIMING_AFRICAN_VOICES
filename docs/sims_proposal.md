# Maloba: Living History, Real Places — A Pitch to the Department of Tourism

**Prepared by:** Maloba team (3-person team, incl. Tumo)
**Purpose:** Partnership proposal — Department of Tourism

---

## 1. The core idea, in one line

Maloba tells the real history of South Africa's places — in the voices of the people who lived it or
the literature that recorded it — and turns every story into a doorway to an actual place you can
visit, and a real local business you can book.

Learn about June 16 → book a bike tour through Soweto. Learn about Mandela → discover Vilakazi
Street, where he lived, and the museums around it. History isn't a static lesson here — it's an
on-ramp to travel.

---

## 2. What Maloba actually does

### a) Real history, place by place — not a forced narrative

Every story in Maloba is tied to a specific, real place. There is no single linear storyline the user
has to follow start to finish. A user can enter through whatever pulls them in — a date, a person, an
event, a topic — and explore outward from there. The history is accurate and sourced; the structure is
exploratory, matching how people actually engage with curiosity and travel planning.

### b) Told by real people and real literature — not AI narration

The history itself comes from two sources, and only these two:

- **Oral history from the people who hold it** — community members, elders, local historians —
  collected with full, explicit informed consent (POPIA-compliant) before any recording happens.
- **Existing published literature**, used only where we have permission or clear licensing to use it —
  not summarized or reworded in a way that misrepresents the source.

AI is not used to generate the historical narrative or the visuals depicting it. Where AI is used at
all, it is limited to translation and language accessibility (see §5) — never to invent or illustrate
history. This is a deliberate integrity line: the credibility of the platform depends on every story
being traceable to a real person or a real, citable source. Anything uncertain is marked and not
presented as fact.

### c) Every story links to a real, bookable place

This is the mechanism that makes Maloba a tourism product, not just an education product. Each
historical entry is tagged to one or more **real-world, visitable, sellable locations and
experiences** — for example:

| Learn about... | Links to... |
|---|---|
| June 16 / the Soweto Uprising | A guided bike tour through Soweto |
| Nelson Mandela | Vilakazi Street (where he lived), Mandela House Museum |
| A historical event or figure tied to a museum | The relevant museum's visit information / booking |

The app is not just telling you a story and leaving you there — it is handing you the exact next step
to go experience it in person, through a real local operator, guide, or heritage site.

---

## 3. Why this is a tourism proposal, not just an app

Our ask to the Department of Tourism is built on this framing: **Maloba educates people about South
Africa's history, and in doing so, actively facilitates travel to the country's heritage sites.**

This is not incidental — it's the core mechanism of the product. The path is:

**Curiosity about history → real place → real local business → an actual visit and spend.**

That is a direct, traceable contribution to heritage tourism, not a general cultural or educational
side-benefit. Every story in the app is designed to end in a bookable action, not just a read.

---

## 4. Why this complements existing tourism initiatives

South African Tourism's **Sho't Left** campaign has spent years building the infrastructure and
audience for domestic travel — deals, listings, a partner platform for tourism businesses, and a
proven message: South Africans and visitors alike often don't realize what's around them and need a
reason to go. Sho't Left's current content leans toward adventure, beach, and general "hidden gem"
deals; it does not currently center history or heritage as the reason to travel.

Maloba supplies exactly that missing reason. Where Sho't Left answers "where can I get a good deal,"
Maloba answers "why should I go, and what does this place mean" — and then routes the user into a
bookable experience. The two are complementary, not competing: Maloba can drive intent and discovery;
existing platforms and local operators handle the booking and commercial relationship.

---

## 5. What supports this model technically (kept brief — not the focus of this pitch)

- **Offline-first app** (WatermelonDB + Supabase sync) — works in low-connectivity areas, relevant for
  travel use.
- **Language accessibility** — translation and indigenous-language support (Setswana, English, and
  others), so stories are accessible in the languages communities actually speak.
- **POPIA-compliant consent flow** for any oral history or personal data collected from contributors.
- **One codebase** (Expo/React Native) covering web, Android, and iOS.

---

## 6. The business/partnership layer

Each linked location or experience represents a potential partner relationship:

- **Tour operators** (e.g. Soweto bike tours) — referral or booking-integration partnerships.
- **Museums and heritage sites** (e.g. Hector Pieterson Museum, Mandela House) — visibility and
  visitor-routing partnerships, potentially co-branded content.
- **Local guides and community contributors** — a path to include and credit the people whose oral
  history populates the app, and potentially compensate them as contributors.

This gives the Department of Tourism a concrete story to tell: Maloba doesn't just promote South
Africa's history — it visibly routes visitors and spend toward specific local heritage businesses and
community contributors.

## 8. Suggested pilot scope

Rather than launching all nine provinces at once, we propose starting with a small number of deeply
covered locations (e.g. Soweto, plus one or two additional heritage-dense areas) to prove the
history-to-booking mechanism works, before scaling city by city.
