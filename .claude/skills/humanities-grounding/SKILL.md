# Humanities Grounding — truth only, no invented heritage

This is the project's non-negotiable rule. Ubuntu Heritage is about real people's history. Fabrication is a
harm, not just a bug. (See [AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md) §2 and
[docs/04-humanities-sources.md](../../../docs/04-humanities-sources.md).)

## When writing or editing content, you MUST:

1. **Trace every fact to a real source** — the text itself or a citation in
   [docs/09-research-summary.md](../../../docs/09-research-summary.md). Put the citation in the scene's
   `sourceNote`.
2. **Never invent** a character, event, date, custom, law, or quote to "fill a gap." If a needed fact
   isn't sourced, write `[NEEDS SOURCE]` and stop — do not guess.
3. **Child Mode adapts tone, never facts.** Simplified text stays faithful: no new events, no softened
   history, no added drama.
4. **Translations are drafts.** Machine-assisted Setswana/other-language text is a draft for human
   review, never presented as authoritative.
5. **AI images are interpretations, not evidence.** Label generated visuals as artistic
   interpretations; never present one as a historical photograph or a real person.
6. **Handle hard material honestly** (e.g. the "Frog's Bride" forced-marriage custom in Mutwa) — with
   context, neither sanitised nor sensationalised.
7. **Credit the author + source** in every module and on the "About the Sources" screen.

## The four canonical sources (don't drift from these)

- **Mhudi** — Sol Plaatje, 1930. First English novel by a Black South African. "The Forest Home";
  Mhudi's proto-feminist agency; subverts the colonial pastoral myth.
- **Ityala Lamawele** — S.E.K. Mqhayi, 1914. Twins Wele & Babini; the *inkundla*; *ubulungisa* &
  *umthetho*; restorative justice.
- **Indaba, My Children** — Credo Mutwa, 1964. Ninavanhu-Ma & the Tree of Life; cosmology; initiation
  rites.
- **Inkondlo kaZulu** — B.W. Vilakazi, 1935. *izimbongi* oral praise poetry → written verse;
  preservation meta-narrative.
