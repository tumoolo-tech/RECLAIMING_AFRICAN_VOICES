# 04 — Humanities Sources (the heart of the project)

This is where the 30% **Humanities Depth** is won or lost. Maloba refuses generic "African history"
summaries. Every module is anchored in a **specific, real, foundational South African text** authored
by an indigenous cultural custodian. The technology is in service of *these* works.

> **Integrity rule (from [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)):** every fact below traces to the source text or a
> citation. When extending content, cite or mark `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — never invent.

---

## Pillar 1 — Sol Plaatje, *Mhudi* (written ~1917, published 1930)

- **Significance:** the **first English-language novel by a Black South African.** Plaatje was a
  polyglot (English, Dutch, German, Sesotho, Zulu) and a founding member of what became the ANC.
- **Setting:** the *Mfecane* and the Matabele (Ndebele) attacks under King Mzilikazi in the 1830s.
- **Why it matters:** it **subverts the colonial pastoral myth** — the landscape is not an empty
  backdrop for European exploration but the stage for complex Barolong politics, diplomacy, and land.
- **Module focus — "The Forest Home" (Chapter 5):** after their village Kunana is destroyed, Mhudi and
  Ra-Thaga build a sanctuary, *Re-Nosi* ("We-are-alone"). Mhudi repeatedly shows superior judgment and
  courage (she saves Ra-Thaga from a lion) — a **proto-feminist, egalitarian** partnership that
  contradicts colonial stereotypes of indigenous domesticity.
- **Interaction:** branching survival narrative; contrast Barolong customary marriage *before* the
  invasion vs the egalitarian union forged in the wilderness.
- **Audience:** young adults / adults — resilience, displacement, agency.

## Pillar 2 — S.E.K. Mqhayi, *Ityala Lamawele* ("The Lawsuit of the Twins", 1914)

- **Significance:** a seminal Xhosa text and a **defense of pre-colonial Xhosa law**, which colonial
  authorities routinely disparaged.
- **Setting:** the reign of King Hintsa (1789–1835).
- **The case:** twins **Wele and Babini** dispute their late father's estate and the right of
  succession — complex because they were born the same day. The text richly depicts the *inkundla*
  (traditional court) and the philosophy of *ubulungisa* (justice) and *umthetho* (law): evidence,
  cross-examination by councillors, and the midwives' testimony on the custom of finger-cutting that
  identified the second-born — **biometric evidence** for the court.
- **Why it matters:** shows the *inkundla* as a sophisticated apparatus for objective truth and
  **restorative justice** — the twins ultimately humble themselves to honour each other's seniority,
  prioritising **communal harmony over individual victory.**
- **Module focus — Virtual *inkundla*:** the user observes/participates in the court, weighs evidence
  and custom.
- **Audience:** academic + general — interactive logic, historical jurisprudence.

## Pillar 3 — Credo Mutwa, *Indaba, My Children* (1964)

- **Significance:** Vusamazulu Credo Mutwa, a Zulu *sangoma*/*sanusi*, controversially broke a vow of
  secrecy to **write down oral tradition**, fearing its erasure under colonialism and apartheid. The
  cosmological + spiritual foundation of Southern Africa.
- **Content:** the creation myth of **Ninavanhu-Ma** (the Great Mother) and the Tree of Life;
  migrations of Bantu-speaking peoples; initiation rites, warrior codes, marital customs.
- **Handle with care:** it does **not** sanitise history — e.g. the **"Frog's Bride"** (a forced
  marriage). Present such material **honestly and with context**, never sensationalised, never hidden.
- **Module focus — "Myth & Origin" visual novel:** cinematic depictions of the cosmology (ideal for
  generative visuals), framed as artistic interpretation.
- **Audience:** children + adults — visually driven, mythological storytelling.
- **Rights — in copyright, not public domain (issue #34, 2026-09-19).** Mutwa died on 25 March 2020;
  under the Copyright Act 98 of 1978 (life + 50) the work is protected until the end of 2070. The
  project's basis for the module is that its two scenes **summarise the book's ideas and the author's
  stated purpose in the project's own words**, cite their sources, and reproduce no passage (the only
  borrowed phrase is the three-word title); the Heritage Ledger fingerprints the module, not the book.
  That is the project's stated basis, not a legal opinion. **Proper next step:** approach the Mutwa
  estate / publisher for permission or a partnership — which is also the right story to tell about a
  living tradition. Until then, no verbatim quotation from the book is added to the module, and the
  Ingestion Library (`services/ingest/rights.ts`) refuses it by construction.

## Pillar 4 — B.W. Vilakazi, *Inkondlo kaZulu* (1935)

- **Significance:** Vilakazi transitioned Zulu **izimbongi** (oral praise poetry) into written classical
  verse — guaranteeing posterity while wrestling with how to keep the oral essence on the page. The
  first published volume of Zulu poetry.
- **Why it matters here:** a **meta-narrative on preservation itself** — how African language adapted
  to the printing press, and by extension how it must now adapt to the digital interface (exactly what
  Maloba does).
- **Module focus:** audio-visual praise-poetry readings; the journey from oral to written word.
- **Audience:** literature enthusiasts; language-preservation advocates.
